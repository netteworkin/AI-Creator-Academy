## Introduction
Why prompt frameworks matter.

Artificial Intelligence is capable of producing remarkable results, but those results depend heavily on the instructions it receives. In the previous chapter, you learned the six building blocks of an effective prompt. While those components improve clarity, experienced AI users often rely on structured prompt frameworks to make their interactions even more consistent and repeatable.

A prompt framework is a proven pattern for organizing instructions. Rather than starting from a blank page each time, you use a framework that guides the AI toward the type of response you need. Think of it as following a recipe instead of guessing the ingredients. The framework provides structure, while your creativity supplies the unique ideas.

Professional writers, marketers, educators, software developers, and entrepreneurs frequently use prompt frameworks because they reduce ambiguity, improve efficiency, and produce more reliable results. As your projects become more complex, these frameworks become even more valuable.

In this chapter, you will explore several widely used prompt frameworks, understand when each one is most effective, and learn how to select the right approach for different tasks. By the end of the chapter, you will have a practical toolkit that you can immediately apply to writing, business, education, research, and content creation.

## Framework 1: RTF
### What Is RTF?

RTF stands for **Role, Task, and Format**. It is one of the simplest and most effective prompt frameworks for beginners because it organizes instructions into three clear components. Instead of giving AI a vague request, you define who the AI should be, what you want it to accomplish, and how you want the response presented.

The RTF framework reduces ambiguity and helps produce more consistent, useful results. Because of its simplicity, it serves as an excellent starting point for anyone learning prompt engineering.

---

### How RTF Works

**Role**

Assign the AI a specific identity or area of expertise.

Examples:

- Marketing Strategist
- Business Coach
- Teacher
- Content Writer
- Software Developer

Giving the AI a role provides context and influences the perspective from which it responds.

---

**Task**

Clearly describe what you want the AI to do.

Examples:

- Write an article
- Explain a concept
- Analyze data
- Create a lesson plan
- Develop a marketing strategy

A well-defined task improves focus and reduces misunderstandings.

---

**Format**

Specify how the response should be organized.

Examples:

- Bullet list
- Table
- Markdown
- Checklist
- Numbered steps
- Executive summary

Choosing the format makes the output easier to use immediately without significant editing.

---

### Example

**Basic Prompt**

Write about artificial intelligence.

**RTF Prompt**

Role:
You are an experienced technology educator.

Task:
Explain artificial intelligence to someone with no technical background.

Format:
Create a beginner-friendly article using headings, bullet points, and a short summary.

Notice how the second prompt provides clear guidance, resulting in a more structured and useful response.

---

### Business Applications

The RTF framework is valuable for:

- Writing blog posts
- Creating marketing content
- Drafting emails
- Producing lesson plans
- Building digital products
- Summarizing research
- Brainstorming ideas

Because it is simple and adaptable, RTF is often the first framework professionals teach to new AI users.

---

### Best Practices

- Choose a specific role.
- Define one clear task.
- Specify the desired output format.
- Keep instructions concise.
- Test and refine your prompts over time.

---

### Common Mistakes

- Assigning multiple conflicting roles.
- Using vague tasks.
- Forgetting to specify the output format.
- Requesting too many objectives in a single prompt.

## Framework 2: CRISPE
### What Is CRISPE?

CRISPE is a comprehensive prompt framework designed for tasks that require detailed instructions and highly structured responses. While RTF focuses on three essential elements, CRISPE expands the process by encouraging you to provide richer context, define expectations more clearly, and guide the AI toward a specific style of response.

Because of its flexibility, CRISPE is especially useful for professional work, business planning, consulting, research, and long-form content creation.

---

### The CRISPE Components

**Capacity**

Define the expertise or capability you want the AI to demonstrate.

Example:

"You are an experienced digital marketing consultant."

---

**Role**

Clarify the perspective or responsibility the AI should assume during the task.

Example:

"Act as my business advisor."

---

**Insight**

Provide important background information that helps the AI understand the situation.

Examples include:

- Business goals
- Target audience
- Industry
- Existing challenges
- Available resources

---

**Statement**

Clearly describe the specific objective.

Example:

"Develop a 90-day content marketing strategy."

---

**Personality**

Specify the tone or communication style.

Examples:

- Professional
- Friendly
- Motivational
- Analytical
- Educational

---

**Experiment**

Invite the AI to suggest alternatives, improvements, or creative ideas rather than producing only one answer.

Example:

"Provide three different strategies and explain the advantages of each."

---

### Example

**Basic Prompt**

Create a marketing plan.

**CRISPE Prompt**

Capacity:
Experienced Digital Marketing Consultant

Role:
Business Growth Advisor

Insight:
A new online business selling AI education to beginners with a limited advertising budget.

Statement:
Develop a 90-day marketing plan focused on organic growth.

Personality:
Professional and encouraging.

Experiment:
Provide three different strategies ranked by difficulty and expected results.

The additional structure helps the AI generate a more thoughtful and customized response.

---

### Business Applications

CRISPE works especially well for:

- Business planning
- Consulting
- Strategic decision-making
- Marketing campaigns
- Product launches
- Course creation
- Long-form reports

---

### Best Practices

- Provide meaningful background information.
- Be specific about the desired outcome.
- Encourage multiple solutions when appropriate.
- Keep the objective focused.
- Review and refine your prompts after testing.

---

### Common Mistakes

- Providing too little context.
- Mixing multiple unrelated objectives.
- Forgetting to define the desired communication style.
- Asking for unrealistic levels of detail without sufficient background information.

---

## Framework 3: Few-Shot Prompting
### What Is Few-Shot Prompting?

Few-Shot Prompting is a technique that teaches an AI how to respond by providing one or more examples before asking it to complete a similar task. Instead of relying only on instructions, you demonstrate the style, structure, or format you want the AI to follow.

This approach is especially effective when you need consistent formatting, a specific writing style, or repeated outputs across multiple tasks.

---

### How Few-Shot Prompting Works

The process is straightforward:

1. Provide one or more examples.
2. Show the pattern you want.
3. Ask the AI to generate a new response using the same pattern.

The AI uses your examples as a guide rather than guessing your expectations.

---

### Example

**Example 1**

Question:
Summarize the following article in three bullet points.

Answer:

- Main idea
- Supporting detail
- Final takeaway

---

**Example 2**

Question:
Summarize this customer review.

Answer:

- Positive experience
- Product benefit
- Overall recommendation

---

**Your Task**

Summarize the following blog post using the same three-bullet structure.

Because the AI has already seen the desired format, the output is usually more consistent.

---

### Business Applications

Few-Shot Prompting works well for:

- Product descriptions
- Social media captions
- Customer support replies
- Blog summaries
- Email templates
- Frequently asked questions
- Standard operating procedures

---

### Best Practices

- Use clear examples.
- Keep the examples consistent.
- Demonstrate the exact format you expect.
- Avoid mixing multiple styles within the same prompt.

---

### Common Mistakes

- Providing inconsistent examples.
- Using examples that are too complex.
- Including unnecessary information.
- Giving examples that conflict with one another.

---

## Framework 4: Persona Prompting
### What Is Persona Prompting?

Persona Prompting is a technique that improves AI responses by assigning the AI a specific identity, profession, or perspective before giving it a task. Rather than asking for a generic answer, you define *who* the AI should be, allowing it to tailor its language, priorities, and recommendations accordingly.

This approach is especially valuable when you want advice, explanations, or content that reflects the knowledge and experience of a particular type of professional.

---

### How Persona Prompting Works

Instead of asking:

"How can I improve my website?"

You might ask:

"You are an experienced UX designer. Review my website and recommend five improvements that would make it easier for first-time visitors to navigate."

The assigned persona helps shape the AI's response, making it more focused and relevant.

---

### Common Personas

Examples include:

- Marketing Strategist
- Business Coach
- Financial Advisor
- Teacher
- Software Engineer
- Graphic Designer
- Copywriter
- Customer Service Manager
- Project Manager
- Career Coach

Choosing the right persona helps the AI emphasize the knowledge and priorities associated with that role.

---

### Example

**Basic Prompt**

Help me create an online course.

**Persona Prompt**

You are an experienced instructional designer who has created successful online courses for beginner learners.

Help me design a six-module course that teaches new entrepreneurs how to use AI to create digital products. Include learning objectives, suggested activities, and assessment ideas.

Notice how assigning a persona results in more structured and educational recommendations.

---

### Business Applications

Persona Prompting is useful for:

- Business consulting
- Marketing strategy
- Course development
- Website reviews
- Resume writing
- Career coaching
- Product design
- Customer support
- Content editing

---

### Best Practices

- Choose one clear persona.
- Match the persona to the task.
- Provide enough context about your project.
- Combine Persona Prompting with other frameworks when appropriate.
- Experiment with different personas to compare responses.

---

### Common Mistakes

- Assigning multiple conflicting personas.
- Choosing a persona unrelated to the task.
- Forgetting to provide project context.
- Expecting the persona alone to solve vague instructions.

## Framework 5: Chain-of-Thought
### What Is Chain-of-Thought Prompting?

Chain-of-Thought Prompting is a framework that encourages AI to approach complex problems by breaking them into smaller, logical steps. Instead of asking for an immediate answer, you guide the AI through a structured reasoning process.

This technique is especially useful for planning, analysis, decision-making, troubleshooting, and any task that requires careful thinking rather than a quick response.

---

### How Chain-of-Thought Works

Rather than asking:

"Create a business."

Guide the AI through individual steps.

Example:

1. Identify the target audience.
2. Define the customer's biggest problem.
3. Brainstorm possible solutions.
4. Evaluate each solution.
5. Recommend the strongest business idea.
6. Create a launch plan.

Breaking a large challenge into smaller pieces often produces more thoughtful and organized results.

---

### Example

**Basic Prompt**

Create a digital product business.

**Chain-of-Thought Prompt**

You are an experienced business strategist.

Help me build an online business by working through the following steps:

1. Identify three profitable niches.
2. Evaluate each niche.
3. Select the strongest opportunity.
4. Suggest three digital products.
5. Recommend a pricing strategy.
6. Outline a 90-day launch plan.

Explain your reasoning at each step before moving to the next.

---

### Business Applications

Chain-of-Thought Prompting is valuable for:

- Business planning
- Research projects
- Strategic decision-making
- Course development
- Project management
- Financial planning
- Process improvement
- Product roadmaps
- Complex problem solving

---

### Best Practices

- Break large problems into manageable steps.
- Keep each step focused on one objective.
- Review each stage before moving forward.
- Build on previous answers rather than starting over.
- Refine the process as new information becomes available.

---

### Common Mistakes

- Combining too many tasks into one step.
- Skipping important stages.
- Asking for conclusions before sufficient analysis.
- Using Chain-of-Thought for very simple tasks where a direct prompt would be more efficient.

> ## Framework Comparison

Choosing the right prompt framework depends on the task you want to accomplish. While every framework can improve AI interactions, each has strengths that make it better suited for certain situations.

| Framework | Best For | Difficulty | Primary Strength |
|-----------|----------|------------|------------------|
| **RTF** | Everyday writing, blogging, emails, simple business tasks | ⭐ Beginner | Fast, clear, and easy to learn |
| **CRISPE** | Strategy, consulting, business planning, detailed reports | ⭐⭐ Intermediate | Rich context and highly customized responses |
| **Few-Shot Prompting** | Templates, repetitive tasks, standardized outputs | ⭐⭐ Intermediate | Consistent formatting and style |
| **Persona Prompting** | Expert advice, coaching, specialized content | ⭐ Beginner | Focused responses from a chosen perspective |
| **Chain-of-Thought Prompting** | Complex planning, analysis, research, decision-making | ⭐⭐⭐ Advanced | Step-by-step reasoning for challenging problems |

### Key Insight

No single framework is "best." The most effective AI users understand the strengths of each framework and select the one that best matches the task at hand. As your experience grows, you'll often combine multiple frameworks within the same prompt to achieve even better results.

## Choosing the Right Framework
Choosing the right prompt framework is much like choosing the right tool from a toolbox. While a hammer is excellent for driving nails, it is not the best choice for tightening a screw. Likewise, different prompt frameworks excel in different situations.

Use **RTF** when you need a simple, structured prompt for everyday writing, content creation, or communication.

Choose **CRISPE** when your project requires detailed context, strategic thinking, or professional recommendations.

Apply **Few-Shot Prompting** whenever consistency is important and you can demonstrate the pattern you want the AI to follow.

Use **Persona Prompting** when you want the AI to respond from the perspective of a particular expert or professional.

Select **Chain-of-Thought Prompting** for projects that involve planning, analysis, troubleshooting, or solving complex problems through logical steps.

As your prompt engineering skills develop, you'll discover that combining these frameworks often produces the strongest results. For example, you might assign an expert persona, provide examples using Few-Shot Prompting, and ask the AI to reason through a challenge step by step using Chain-of-Thought Prompting.

## Common Mistakes
Even well-designed prompt frameworks can produce disappointing results if they are used incorrectly. Fortunately, most mistakes are easy to identify and fix with practice.

### 1. Being Too Vague

AI performs best when instructions are specific and clearly defined.

Instead of writing:

"Help me market my business."

Try:

"Create a 30-day social media content plan for a new online business that teaches beginners how to use AI."

---

### 2. Overloading a Single Prompt

Avoid asking the AI to complete too many unrelated tasks at once. Large requests are often easier to manage when divided into smaller prompts or organized with a framework such as Chain-of-Thought Prompting.

---

### 3. Ignoring Context

The more relevant background information you provide, the more tailored the AI's response can become.

Include information such as:

- Target audience
- Goals
- Constraints
- Industry
- Experience level

---

### 4. Forgetting the Desired Format

Always tell the AI how you want the response presented.

Examples include:

- Table
- Checklist
- Bullet list
- Executive summary
- Lesson plan

---

### 5. Never Refining the Prompt

Prompt engineering is an iterative process. Small adjustments often produce significant improvements.

>
> Save your best prompts in a personal prompt library. Over time, you'll build a collection of reusable templates that save hours of work.

- Too little context
- Too many instructions
- Conflicting constraints
- Undefined audience
- Poor formatting
